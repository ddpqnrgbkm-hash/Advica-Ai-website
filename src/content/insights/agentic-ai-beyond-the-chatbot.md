For the last couple of years, most people's experience of AI has been: ask a question, get an answer. That's changing. The focus is shifting from AI that *talks* to AI that *does* — often called "agentic" AI.

## From reporting to doing

The difference is easiest to see with an example.

- **Traditional AI or reporting:** tells you your supply chain is running slowly.
- **Agentic AI:** spots the bottleneck, contacts the supplier for an update and drafts a revised order for you to approve.

The person stays in charge. The AI does the legwork.

## Why control matters

Businesses are increasingly cautious about sending sensitive data to general-purpose public AI tools. Agentic systems can be built to run in a controlled environment — inside your own cloud, with clear limits on what they can see and do, and a record of every action taken.

## Where agentic AI helps

Good candidates are tasks that are repetitive, follow clear rules and involve several systems, for example:

- Chasing and reconciling invoices
- Triaging customer emails and preparing replies
- Pulling information from documents into your systems
- Preparing weekly reports from several data sources
