The AI headlines tend to focus on the biggest models. But in boardrooms, the conversation is shifting: for many business tasks, bigger isn't better. Small AI models are often the smarter choice.

## The trouble with "big"

When you plug a huge, general-purpose model into your business, you inherit a black box. You have little visibility of how it was trained, running costs can climb quickly, and responses can be slower than you'd like. For businesses handling sensitive records, that can be a governance headache.

## The rise of small language models

Small language models (SLMs) are focused, efficient and can run within your own infrastructure. They're well suited to specific, well-defined jobs such as classifying documents, extracting information or answering questions from a set of company documents.

- **Control.** You can decide what data they learn from and keep decisions inside your own systems.
- **Efficiency.** They can run on your own servers, or even on devices, quickly and at lower cost.

## When to choose small

Small models are a good fit when the task is narrow, the data is sensitive or you need predictable costs at scale. Larger models still have their place for broad, open-ended work. Often the best answer is a mix of both.
