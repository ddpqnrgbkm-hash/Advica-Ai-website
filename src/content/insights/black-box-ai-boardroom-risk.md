The "move fast and break things" era of AI is ending. As regulation tightens and customers ask harder questions, the message for business leaders is simple: if you can't explain or audit what your AI is doing, you shouldn't be relying on it for important decisions.

## A prompt is not a strategy

Many businesses' first steps with AI have been simple tools sitting on top of public AI services. That's a fine way to experiment, but for anything involving sensitive data or important decisions, you need more control.

The direction of travel is towards AI you control: owning the business logic, knowing where your data lives and keeping sensitive information within boundaries you set.

## Three principles for AI you can trust

AI is only as good as the foundations it sits on. Moving away from the "black box" means focusing on three things:

1. **Auditable logic.** Every important decision an AI system makes should be traceable, so you and your auditors can see why it happened.
2. **Control over your data.** Sensitive data should stay within your own cloud account or approved services, under rules you decide.
3. **Reliability.** AI systems need the same testing, monitoring and engineering discipline as any other business-critical software.

## Where to start

You don't need to rebuild everything at once. Start by listing where AI is already used in your business, what data it touches and who checks its output. That simple map often reveals the quickest wins, and the biggest risks.
